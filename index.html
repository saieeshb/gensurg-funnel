STAGE 01

Applications: past about sixty, you are buying nothing

Applicants over-invest here more than anywhere else, and it is the only stage where spending more money reliably makes the numbers worse.

Verdict

Applying to more programmes does not get you more interviews. Across 346 applicants, the correlation between applications sent and interviews received is negative (ρ = ), and the correlation with hit rate is strongly negative (ρ = ). Below 60 applications each additional ten buys about +1.9 interviews. Between 61 and 120 the marginal return is zero.

n = 346 applicants Spearman ρ, p < 10⁻³⁰ This is confounded — read how

What this does not say

It does not say that cutting your list to 40 programmes will raise your hit rate. Your hit rate is mostly a property of your application. What it does say is that the marginal application past roughly sixty is close to worthless, and the money and effort it costs are better spent on the things Stage 02 rewards.

STAGE 02

Invites: this is where the cycle is decided

Between 75 applications and 14 invites sits the only filter that matters. Four things move it, and they are not what applicants talk about most.

Verdict

Signals are the strongest lever you control, and they are worth roughly 3.9× on the odds of an invitation. But a signal amplifies a strong application rather than rescuing a weak one: signal conversion rises steeply with Step 2 CK, from 4.0 of 15 below 240 to 9.1 of 15 above 270.

Tier-matched odds ratio 3.85 95% CI 1.77–8.35 How the denominator was recovered

“For our program, no change in the number of interviews. However, it looks like a greater fraction of our invites are going to students that signaled. We had more students signal than interview spots.”

Program director · PCPD Q&A tab, 2025–26 workbook

If you are at zero on release day

Signal share among invitees peaks at 60.6% during release week and falls to 27.8% by mid-November. Unsignalled invitations arrive disproportionately off the peak. A quiet 21 October feels like a verdict and is not one; the season runs another three months.

STAGE 03 — 04

Interviews and the match: the count is nearly the whole story

By the time invitations are in hand, the outcome is close to determined. That is why Stage 02 deserves the effort Stage 01 usually absorbs.

Verdict

Interview count dominates every other measured factor: 9.1× the odds of a categorical match per five additional interviews. Applicants with ten or more invitations matched categorical 91% of the time; those with two or fewer, 0%.

ACROSS CYCLES

What two years can and cannot tell you

The two workbooks record different things. Treating them as a time series is the easiest mistake to make with this data, so the comparisons below stay inside what is genuinely comparable.

The structural difference

The 2024–25 workbook is applicant-level: it knows who you are, what you scored, and what happened to you — but it has no dated invitation log, so it can say nothing about timing. The 2025–26 workbook is invite-level: every invitation is dated and attributed to a programme, but no invitation can be linked to an applicant and no outcome is recorded. Each answers what the other cannot, which is why they are read side by side here rather than joined into a trend.

DECISIONS

What to actually do differently

Ordered by how much the evidence supports them, with the evidence named. Where the data is weak, it says so.

Spend signals where you fit, not where you would be flattered to go

A signal is worth about 3.9× on the odds. At the most sought-after programmes 75% of invitees had signalled, so an unsignalled application there competes for a quarter of the seats. At programmes almost nobody signals, the same signal is a much rarer object.

strong evidenceTier-matched OR 3.85 (1.77–8.35)Method

Do not spend a signal on your home programme

Home-programme invitees signalled at the base rate, 50.0% against 54.2% for everyone else, with no visible penalty for skipping it. That is a free signal to redeploy.

strong evidenceOR 0.85, p = 0.64 · n = 1,142Method

Stop adding programmes past about sixty

Marginal return falls from +1.9 interviews per ten applications to zero. Redirect the fee money and the supplemental-essay hours into the applications you have already sent.

moderate · confoundedρ = −0.57, n = 346How

If your score is your weak point, rotate rather than signal harder

Signals do not compensate for a low score — conversion falls with score, it does not rise. An away rotation is different: at least one programme director in the workbook describes keeping a shortlist for people who rotated there, exempt from score screening.

moderater = +0.44 score vs conversion; rotation effect is PD testimony, not measured

Treat publications as the lowest-yield thing on your list

Holding score, pathway, AOA and class rank constant, publication count has no measurable effect on interview count. This is the clearest gap between what applicants optimise and what the data rewards.

strong nullIRR 1.01 (0.93–1.09), n = 337Power caveat

Send the letter of intent; expect nothing from it

34% of letters got any response at all and 30% were personalised. Two independent tallies in the workbooks agree. It is cheap to send and near-useless as a read on where you stand.

moderate42 of 122 responded; two independent tabs agree

If you are a non-US IMG, weight research and advocacy over signals

Signal conversion for IMGs was 2.4 of 15 against 6.5 for US MDs. The applicant in the workbook who reported six categorical invitations with zero signal conversions credited research volume and mentors making calls on their behalf.

Why two candidate dates

22 October 2025 satisfies both plausible rules, “programme access + 28 days” and “the fourth Wednesday of October”, so last cycle cannot distinguish them. In 2026 they diverge by a week. Confirm against the APDS statement and the AAMC ERAS 2027 calendar before planning around either.

LIMITS

What this data cannot do

Read this before you use any number above to make a decision.

Both workbooks are voluntary and self-reported. People post when something happens, so non-events are invisible: an applicant with fifteen invitations can post fifteen times, one with none posts never. Together the two cycles capture roughly 5–6% of national General Surgery interview volume. No absolute national rate can be computed from them, which is why the findings above are comparisons within the data rather than levels.

Records are also not independent — one programme contributes up to 26 invitations, all filtered by a single committee's rule. The intraclass correlation is 0.415, which means the effective sample size is about 465, not 1,154. Every model above uses programme-clustered standard errors for this reason. And this dataset cannot see effects smaller than about 10 percentage points, so every null result here is a bound, not a proof of absence.
