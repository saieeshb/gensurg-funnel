Methods, and every reason to distrust this

The findings page states what the data supports. This page states how it was obtained and where it breaks. If a number on the other page matters to a decision you are about to make, the reason to trust or discard it is here.

02 · Turning free text into records

The 2025–26 invitation log is a date × programme grid in which each cell holds a programme name followed by one parenthetical per invitee, in a convention the sheet declares in its own header. A real cell, unedited: 

Bayhealth Medical Center (DE)
(-home,-away, -signal, +geo, MD,25x),
(-home, -away, -signal, +geo, IMG, 27x)
(-home, -away, -signal, +geo, DO, 26x)+2, (-home, -away, -signal, +geo, MD, 24x) +4

The parser tolerates unclosed parentheses, { typed for (, tally suffixes (x2 meaning two people, +4 meaning four additional people), six spellings of the IMG pathway, four score formats including the typo 256, and attribute order swaps. It yielded 1,004 parentheticals expanding to 1,154 weighted invitations.

One decision matters more than the rest: missing attributes are coded unknown, not negative. An entry reading (+home, -away, +geo, MD, 24x) has no signal field. Treating that as “no signal” would have manufactured the headline result. Field completeness after parsing ran 97.5–99.9%.

03 · The field Google Sheets destroyed

The 2024–25 workbook has a column headed II by Signals x/15 — how many of an applicant's fifteen programme signals produced an interview. It looked unusable: most cells held dates like 2024-07-15.

They are not dates. Google Sheets silently coerced entries typed as 7/15 into 7 July. The day component is always 15, the denominator; the month component is the numerator. Values above twelve survived as text, because 13/15 is not a valid month. That is the pattern that confirms the mechanism rather than merely fitting it. 

def sig_yield(v):
    if isinstance(v, (datetime, date, pd.Timestamp)):
        return v.month if v.day == 15 else nan   # "7/15" -> 2024-07-15
    m = re.match(r'^(\d{1,2})\s*/\s*15$', str(v).strip())
    return float(m.group(1)) if m else nan       # "13/15" survived as text

This recovered 254 usable records, 233 of them rescued from date-coercion. It is the only direct measurement of signal conversion in either workbook, and the entire signal-amplification finding rests on it.

04 · The denominator problem

This is the analysis most readings of these spreadsheets get wrong. The invitation log records only invitations. You cannot compute the probability of an interview given a signal from invitations alone. Observing that 54% of invitees signalled proves nothing on its own: if 54% of applications carried signals, the lift would be exactly zero.

The denominator came from Comm 25-26, a tab nobody treats as an analytic asset. It tallies, per programme, how many sheet users applied and how many applied without a signal. Across 17 programmes and 544 applications, 44.1% carried a signal, and the rate varies enormously by programme: 10.7% at Orlando Health, 64.9% at Baylor.

The estimate was then made two independent ways, which is why it is trustworthy:

  • Tier-matched. Nine programmes appear in both tabs. Same programmes, same cohort, both sides carrying the same reporting bias. Applications: 45.6% signalled. Invitations from those same programmes: 76.3%. Odds ratio 3.85 (95% CI 1.77–8.35).
  • Structural. Fifteen signals divided by N applications gives a base rate directly. At the 60–70 applications typical of this specialty, the implied odds ratio is 3.50–4.28.

Two methods with different assumptions and different failure modes landing on the same number is worth more than either alone.

05 · Clustering, and why n is not 1,154

Invitation records are not independent. One programme contributes up to 26 of them, all produced by one committee applying one screening rule. 91 programmes contribute five or more, holding 56% of the data.

An ICC of 0.415 is itself a finding: programmes differ from each other far more than applicants within a programme differ. Which programme you apply to is a bigger lever than marginal changes to your application. Every model on the findings page uses programme-clustered standard errors.

The limitation that cannot be fixed

Applicant identity is recorded nowhere in the 2025–26 invitation log. One applicant with twelve invitations appears as twelve independent records and there is no way to detect it. No correction exists for this, and it is the single largest unfixable weakness in the invite-side analysis.

The first casualty was a hypothesis this analysis expected to confirm: that low scorers lean on signals to compensate. Among invitees, they do not. The 2024–25 conversion data then showed the opposite relationship, with conversion rising alongside score, and that is the finding which replaced it.

07 · Selection bias, named and directed

Against roughly 1,700–1,800 categorical positions and 10–12 interviews offered per position, national volume is around 18,000–22,000 invitations. This dataset holds 1,154, so coverage is about 5–6%. That figure rests on external anchors rather than the workbooks.

This is why the load-bearing estimate is the tier-matched one: both sides of that comparison are drawn from the same self-selected population, so the bias largely cancels. Comparisons within the data are defensible. Levels are not.

08 · Confounding in the application-count result

The finding that hit rate collapses as application count rises is not causal and cannot be made causal with this data. The obvious mechanism runs backwards: applicants who expect a low hit rate apply more broadly because they expect it. Application count is partly a measure of the applicant's own assessment of their competitiveness.

What survives that objection is narrower, and is the version stated on the findings page:

  • the marginal slope flattens to zero past roughly sixty applications, within the observed data;
  • the broad-application strategy demonstrably does not rescue the people using it — the 120+ group ends with fewer interviews in absolute terms than the 40–59 group;
  • therefore the marginal application past sixty has a poor expected return, whichever direction the causation runs.

It does not follow that cutting a list to forty programmes raises anyone's hit rate. Nothing here supports that, and it would be the natural misreading.

09 · Why outcome reporting is the weakest link

Only 185 of 361 applicants (51%) recorded a match outcome. Reporting is voluntary and happens months after the fact. People who match have reason to return and update; people who do not match also return, to ask what to do next. The direction of this bias is genuinely unclear, which is worse than a bias whose direction is known.

The 65.4% categorical rate on the findings page is therefore a within-sample figure and should not be read as a match rate for General Surgery. The interview-count gradient survives this better than the level does, because it compares reporters against reporters.

One further caution on the match model: conditional on interview count, Step 2 CK flips negative (OR 0.58, p = 0.02). That is not evidence a higher score hurts. It is collider stratification — holding interview count fixed compares a 270 who managed only eight interviews against a 240 who managed eight, and the former has something else going wrong or aimed at harder programmes. The causal path for score runs through interview count, where the Poisson model measures it at 1.22× per ten points.

10 · What this dataset cannot see

Effects below about ten percentage points are invisible here. Every null result on the findings page (publications, geographic tie in the invite model, home rotation, DO status) is consistent with a real effect under that threshold. This is absence of evidence. The publications result says publication count is not a large lever for interview count. It does not say the effect is zero.

11 · Model specifications

Interview count (2024–25)

Poisson GLM, HC1 robust standard errors, n = 337. Outcome: number of interview invitations. Predictors: Step 2 CK centred at 255 per 10 points, IMG pathway, AOA, M3 surgery honours, log publications, top-quartile class rank, log applications sent.

Signalled-invitee status (2025–26)

Logistic GLM, standard errors clustered by programme, n = 1,093 invitations across 306 programmes. Outcome: whether the invitee had signalled. Predictors: Step 2 bucket, geographic tie, home programme, away rotation, weeks into the season, IMG pathway, DO pathway, most-sought programme.

Cox–Snell pseudo-R² is 0.081. Most variance is unexplained, as expected when the strongest real inputs (letters, medical school, the interview itself) go unobserved. A model of this data reporting a high R² would be the suspicious one.

Robustness

  • Dropping 22 October (35% of the invitation data) narrows the tier gradient from +24.6 to +17.0 pp; it survives.
  • Cluster bootstrap over programmes, 4,000 resamples: gap 24.8 pp, 95% CI 8.6–39.4, P(gap ≤ 0) = 0.0013.
  • Simpson's paradox check: the near-zero pooled score/signal correlation does not conceal opposite within-tier signs (+0.015 and −0.052).

A note on colour

The charts use one accent against neutral ink, which fails a categorical-palette validator on lightness and chroma by construction, since near-black is a neutral rather than a hue. The checks governing whether two marks can be told apart all pass (CVD ΔE 26.4, normal-vision ΔE 38.8, contrast ≥3:1). Both two-series charts also separate their series by hatch fill, a legend, and direct median labels, so identity never rests on colour alone.

12 · Reproduce it yourself

Everything on both pages is generated from the two workbooks by the scripts in analysis/. Nothing is hand-entered. 

git clone https://github.com/saieeshb/gensurg-funnel
cd gensurg-funnel
python3 -m venv venv && ./venv/bin/pip install -r analysis/requirements.txt
cd analysis
../venv/bin/python parse_ii.py      # 2025-26 invitations -> records
../venv/bin/python parse_2425.py    # 2024-25 applicants  -> records
../venv/bin/python analyze.py       # composition, base rates, timing
../venv/bin/python analyze2.py      # tiers, geography, IMG, letters
../venv/bin/python analyze3.py      # clustering, bootstrap, projection
../venv/bin/python combined.py      # both cycles together
../venv/bin/python export_site.py   # -> assets/site.json

The scripts expect the two workbooks at the paths in data/. Every figure on the findings page reads assets/site.json, so you can diff a re-run against what is published.
